Fixed Rates Rise Again, But an Opportunity for Some Borrowers

Published Thursday, June 4, 2026 · Rates, market figures and outlooks in this article reflect its publication date and may differ today. Ask us for the latest picture. Translated from the Korean original.

Fixed mortgage rates are climbing again, driven by the recent rise in Government of Canada bond yields. However, for some existing mortgage holders, this is actually having a positive effect by reducing their penalty burden.

Key Points

  • Rising government bond yields increase upward pressure on fixed rates
  • Some lenders have started raising fixed mortgage rates
  • Concerns about inflation and rising oil prices are affecting the bond market
  • Possible higher costs for new borrowers
  • Expected reduction in IRD penalties for existing fixed-rate borrowers

What It Means

Fixed mortgage rates are influenced more directly by bond yields than by the Bank of Canada's policy rate. With bond yields rising recently, fixed rates are also moving up again, which can increase the burden on new buyers and renewal clients. On the other hand, some borrowers with existing fixed-rate mortgages may see the IRD (Interest Rate Differential) penalty for breaking their mortgage early go down as rates rise. Ultimately, the current market structure is becoming clearer: variable rates are relatively stable, while fixed rates move with bond market volatility.
