U.S. June 'Jobs Shock' Eases Rate Hike Pressure: Breathing Room for the Mortgage Market

Published Friday, July 3, 2026 · Rates, market figures and outlooks in this article reflect its publication date and may differ today. Ask us for the latest picture. Translated from the Korean original.

According to the U.S. Department of Labor, non-farm payrolls rose by just 57,000 in June, only about half of what market experts expected, resulting in a 'jobs shock.' This clear signal of a cooling labour market has significantly eased concerns about Federal Reserve (Fed) rate hikes that had been weighing on financial markets.

Key Points

  • New jobs miss expectations: June job growth was only 57,000, a shock well below the expert forecast (115,000).
  • Unemployment rate edges down: Although job gains dropped sharply, the unemployment rate came in at 4.2%, slightly below the market forecast (4.3%).
  • Bond yields fall in response: Right after the slowdown in hiring was confirmed, expectations of further rate hikes quickly faded, and short-term government bond yields immediately fell.
  • Relief rally in asset markets: As rate pressure eased, the Dow rose, and gold prices, which move inversely to interest rates, rebounded.

What It Means

With U.S. employment data, a benchmark for global financial markets, cooling like this, conditions now appear favourable for downward pressure on rates in Canadian financial markets as well. If the relief that the Fed's tightening stance will ease due to the jobs shock is reflected in the bond market, Canadian fixed mortgage rates, which had been rising recently, are more likely to lose upward momentum and gradually stabilize.

However, the details, such as the unemployment rate, still show downward rigidity, so it is more realistic to expect a gradual, range-bound easing rather than a sharp plunge in rates. If you are planning a renewal or a home purchase, we recommend setting aside excessive market anxiety and using this as an opportunity to calmly follow rate movements while reviewing your own cash flow and financial health.
