The Fed's 'Higher for Longer' Warning and the Bank of Canada's (BoC) Dilemma

Published Thursday, April 16, 2026 · Rates, market figures and outlooks in this article reflect its publication date and may differ today. Ask us for the latest picture. Translated from the Korean original.

Recent U.S. inflation data (PPI) and comments from Federal Reserve officials are adding to uncertainty in the Canadian mortgage market. The Bank of Canada (BoC) is now caught in a dilemma with no easy way out.

Key Points

1. U.S.–Canada decoupling: the U.S., where high inflation has become entrenched (cuts delayed), vs. Canada, where domestic demand is cooling, including a drop in GDP (cuts urgently needed)

2. The Fed's hawkish stance: Chicago Fed President Goolsbee suggested "rate cuts could be delayed until 2027"

3. Exchange rate and imported inflation risk: if the BoC cuts rates pre-emptively to stimulate domestic demand, the interest rate gap (spread) between the two countries widens

This risks causing a spike in import prices and reigniting inflation

4. Policy dependence: in the end, despite Canada's own weakening fundamentals, the pace and size of BoC rate cuts will inevitably be held back by the Fed's moves

What It Means

Even if Canada's weak domestic demand partly offsets upward pressure from rising oil prices, a 'currency plunge' caused by monetary policy diverging from the U.S. is a critical risk the BoC would find hard to absorb. Markets are cheering in anticipation of early rate cuts, but the macro reality is cold. Variable rate cuts could come much later than the market expects, and fixed rates, which move with U.S. Treasury yields, are also unlikely to fall meaningfully for the time being. Rather than betting on premature rate cuts, now is the time to take a conservative view and thoroughly review your mortgage renewal strategy and repayment capacity.
