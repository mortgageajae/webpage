Oil Price Shock: A Canadian Rate Hike Scenario Emerges

Published Thursday, March 26, 2026 · Rates, market figures and outlooks in this article reflect its publication date and may differ today. Ask us for the latest picture. Translated from the Korean original.

As the oil price surge from the Middle East revives inflation concerns, the interest rate outlook is shifting quickly. The market has begun pricing in the possibility of a rate hike, moving beyond its previous expectation of a rate hold.

Key Points

1. Middle East geopolitical risk → oil price surge

2. Higher energy prices → possibility of reigniting inflation

3. Markets increasing bets on rate hikes (pricing in up to 0.75% within the year)

4. Central bank signals a possible rate hike if needed

5. Policy dilemma deepens between a rate hold and a hike

What It Means

The market is rapidly changing direction: "expectations of rate cuts → hold → possible hikes again." Higher oil prices have both positive and negative effects on the Canadian economy, but if they stoke inflation, they could lead to pressure for rate hikes. For borrowers in particular, this is a period of renewed interest rate volatility, and choosing between a fixed and variable strategy appears to be more important than ever.
