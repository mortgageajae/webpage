Canadian Personal Insolvencies at Highest Level Since 2009: A Warning Light for the Mortgage Market

Published Thursday, May 14, 2026 · Rates, market figures and outlooks in this article reflect its publication date and may differ today. Ask us for the latest picture. Translated from the Korean original.

The number of consumer insolvencies in Canada recently hit its highest level since 2009, when the aftermath of the 2008 financial crisis struck. It is a painful economic indicator showing that, with household debt reaching a breaking point, borrowers are struggling to repay their debts.

Key Points

  • Personal insolvencies in Q1 2026 totalled 37,121, up 8.5% from the same period a year earlier, the highest level in 17 years.
  • As of the end of 2025, total household debt in Canada was approaching $2.6 trillion, and the accumulated interest burden is pushing borrowers to their financial breaking point.
  • With mounting debt stress, the national mortgage delinquency rate in Canada also rose to 0.24% as of Q4 2025, the highest since the end of 2021.
  • It is a structure in which a single small shock (tipping point), such as job loss, rising rent or an unexpected expense, can push someone into insolvency they cannot recover from on their own.

What It Means

The simultaneous rise in insolvencies and mortgage delinquency rates signals that banks and lenders will make their lending guidelines stricter and more conservative going forward. Borrowers facing a renewal or refinance should prepare for these higher qualification hurdles by reviewing their debt service ratios (GDS/TDS) and managing their credit well ahead of maturity; conservative, proactive financial planning is essential at this point.
