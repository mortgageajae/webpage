Fed Chair Nominee Warsh's Hearing: 'Defending Independence' Dampens Hopes for Early Cuts

Published Thursday, April 23, 2026 · Rates, market figures and outlooks in this article reflect its publication date and may differ today. Ask us for the latest picture. Translated from the Korean original.

At Tuesday's confirmation hearing for Fed Chair nominee Kevin Warsh, the bold White House-driven declaration of 'early rate cuts' that the market had quietly hoped for did not come.

Key Points

1. Emphasis on independence: drew a clear line against political pressure, saying he "would never agree to a presidential directive to cut rates"

2. Conditional case for cuts: refused to promise an immediate 'big cut,' but offered a rationale for cutting rates if AI-driven productivity gains bring inflation down

3. Continued QT (quantitative tightening): reaffirmed his intent to shrink the Fed's bloated balance sheet gradually and carefully

4. Focus on the core mandate: rule out involvement in non-core areas such as climate change (ESG) and focus solely on inflation and employment

What Does It Mean?

This hearing was "a delicate balancing act between a White House that wants growth and a Fed that must control inflation." With nominee Warsh holding firm to independence on principle and affirming continued quantitative tightening (QT), the illusion that rates would drop sharply in the near term under political pressure has been shattered. This means long-term bond yields, the benchmark for fixed rates, are unlikely to fall meaningfully for the time being. Rather than relying on hopes for premature early cuts, now is the time for risk management firmly grounded in economic fundamentals and a conservative repayment plan.
